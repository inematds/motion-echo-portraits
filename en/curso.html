A clear face carries the violet echo

You can prepare a reference photo and create your first violet portrait attempt.

A beautiful trail can hide a person you can no longer recognize. Start by protecting the face. Then you can control the effect.

In 1 minute

  1. Use a clear photo you have permission to use.
  2. Ask for violet and just one residual profile.
  3. Compare the generated face with the original photo.

1The reference shows who should remain in the image

A reference photo needs to show the eyes, nose, and mouth. Choose an image of yourself or one you have permission to use. Avoid dark glasses and hands covering the face.

Lia photographs artists for publicity. She chooses an authorized front-facing portrait with the hair and eyes visible.

2The request limits the effect to the area around the face

The motion echo stays behind the main position. Specify a color and a direction for the trail. Don’t ask for several neon lights at once.

Davi is preparing a promotion for a singer. He replaces a generic request with an instruction for a sharp face and a violet echo.

3A beautiful result still needs to be compared

Zoom in on the original photo and the generated version. Check the shape of the eyes, nose, and mouth. If they changed, go back to the original reference. Reduce the effect before trying again.

Lia notices that the nose looks different. She rejects the version, even though she likes the violet color.

Stuck here? That's normalCan’t find where to attach the photo? Look for the image button or the paper clip symbol. The names vary across tools. If this feature isn’t available, save the request in Notes and return to the practice when you have a compatible tool.

Lesson 2 of 4

Cyan dissolves the edges while preserving the front

You can create a cyan attempt with trails outside the center of the face.

Asking for blur without limits can erase the expression. In this lesson, the movement starts at the silhouette. The center of the face stays clear.

In 1 minute

  1. Return to your original photo.
  2. Limit the dissolve effect to the hair and shoulder.
  3. Check whether the trail crossed any facial features.

1The silhouette defines where the effect can begin

The silhouette separates the person from the background. Ask for the effect on the hair and shoulder. Don’t use the whole face as the area for the dissolve effect.

Davi is preparing a portrait for a music profile. He keeps the expression clear and focuses the lines behind the hair.

2One color gives the portrait a unified look

Cyan is a greenish blue. Use it as the dominant light and keep the shadows dark. Light and dark variations of the same color family don’t require other neon colors.

Lia is creating a cool-toned concept for a band. She keeps the cyan and asks for less brightness in the background.

3The trail needs to end before it reaches the facial features

See where the lines begin. If they cross the mouth or eyes, the limit failed. Ask for less intensity and a smaller area. Use the original photo again if the person has changed.

Davi finds lines over the main eye. He limits the effect to the edge of the hair before choosing the image.

Stuck here? That's normalIs the effect too strong? Write: reduce the dissolve and apply it only to the hair outline. Make one change per attempt.

Lesson 3 of 4

Scarlet gains strength with a controlled trail

You can create a scarlet attempt and correct excessive dragging.

An intense effect doesn't need to fill the whole image. The contrast appears when part of the portrait stays sharp. You'll choose where to increase the strength.

In 1 minute

  1. Use the original photo as a reference.
  2. Choose a warm family: red and amber.
  3. Change only one feature per attempt.

1The drag appears at the edge of the figure

Pixel drag looks like small stretched fragments. Ask for this effect at the edge of the hair or clothing. Keep the main face intact.

Lia is preparing an image for a concert poster. She focuses the drag on the coat to keep the expression recognizable.

2Red and amber form a warm color family

Choose scarlet as the dominant color. Small amber highlights can complement that light. Don’t add cyan or violet in the same attempt. Dark clothing helps the face stand out.

Davi gets a portrait with red, green, and blue lights. He keeps only the red color family for the musician’s promotion.

3One change at a time shows what improved

Changing the color, pose, and trail all at once makes a clear comparison impossible. Choose just one problem. Write down what should stay and what should change.

Lia likes the light, but the hair changed. She opens a new request and attaches only the original photo. Then she repeats the scarlet request and adds the correction.

Stuck here? That's normalNot sure which flaw to fix first? Compare the eyes, nose, and mouth with the reference. Then check the hair and apparent age. Only then adjust the trail.

Blue closes the series with continuous motion

You can create a blue version and select your approved versions for a series.

The final effect looks like a turn captured by a camera. The image is generated, but it needs visual logic. Then you decide which versions really work.

In 1 minute

  1. Use a soft trail, without pixel blocks.
  2. Compare the identity in each version.
  3. Keep only images that meet the criteria.

1The turn leaves a previous position behind

Long exposure is a visual reference for the effect. Ask for one sharp main face and a soft echo. Avoid asking for several people or separate heads.

Davi prepares the final option for the musician. He asks for a continuous turn and makes the second silhouette very faint.

2The template is ready when you choose the options

The request below already uses electric blue and a dark jacket. You can change the outfit to a sweatshirt. Keep one dominant color and avoid changing other details in the same attempt.

Lia wears a dark jacket for the final portrait. She keeps the same original photo that guided the other styles.

3The series needs four individual approvals

Compare each image with the original photo, not just with the other versions. Check the face, color, and direction of motion. If an image fails, redo only that option.

Davi approves three versions and rejects one with altered eyes. He keeps the three and corrects the remaining version.

Stuck here? That's normalHaven't done the earlier lessons? Start with the blue image. Then use the requests from lessons 1, 2, and 3 with the same original reference.
