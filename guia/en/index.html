The face is the anchor. Motion surrounds it.

Motion Echo Portraits is the name of this collection of visual directions for portraits. The supplied material describes a creative technique, not an application you need to install.

The result combines a sharp main expression with trails suggesting an earlier action. A good echo feels part of the same movement; a disconnected duplicate looks like another person in the scene.

Make three decisions: which face to preserve, where the movement goes and which color dominates. Only then add texture. The four English blocks below reproduce the original prompts supplied by the user from the AVCC · AI Video Creators guide. The explanations and exercises are educational additions by INEMA.

Before opening the generator

  • Choose a sharp portrait with visible eyes, nose and mouth. Favor simple clothing, few accessories and a background without distractions.
  • Use a photo of yourself or someone who has authorized this use.
  • Open an image tool that accepts a reference photo and editing instructions. Check whether it supports identity preservation; results vary between tools.
  • Start with a single photo and a single style. Mixing several references makes it harder to identify what caused a change in the face.

Button names vary. Look for the option to attach an image or edit an existing image. A style-only reference field may copy the look without preserving identity.

This guide does not generate images or send your photo to a service. Copy the prompt and practice in your chosen tool.

One change per attempt

Sharp reference→One color→One movement→Face review→Trail adjustment

Use the same photo, framing and expression for all four styles. This lets you compare the effect instead of comparing four different people or poses.

Your first portrait, step by step

1

Attach the reference

Open the editing or reference-based generation feature. Attach only your chosen portrait. Visually confirm it is the right photo before generating.

2

Choose your direction

For a first test, use cyan dissolve: a trail around the edges is easier to assess than multiple face positions. If you prefer another style, use only its corresponding prompt.

3

Copy a complete recipe

Each recipe below includes identity reference instructions and visual direction. Paste the entire text. In the fourth prompt, replace the two bracketed choices with one color and one outfit. Add the negative guardrails at the end; if there is a dedicated negative prompt field, put those restrictions there.

4

Generate and examine the face first

Compare the eyes, nose, mouth, apparent age and hairstyle with the reference. If anything changed, return to the original photo and ask for a weaker effect. Do not use the altered face as a new reference.

5

Correct only what failed

If the identity is correct, adjust only the position or strength of the echo. For example: “Keep the main face and color of this version. Reduce the trail intensity and limit it to the edge of the hair.”

6

Save the version and prompt

Download the approved result and keep the text you used. Record the tool, date and adjustment. Before using it on a cover or poster, check the crop and dimensions required for that use.

Long-exposure shutter turn

Residual face positions that suggest a single continuous turn.

Use the uploaded photo as the face reference. Keep the face, features and hairstyle exactly the same.

Cinematic motion blur portrait, vertical 9:16. The person is turned three-quarters, chin slightly up, eyes closed or looking past the camera. Long-exposure effect: the face stays sharp while a ghosted, horizontally smeared copy of the head and shoulders trails to one side, as if the shutter stayed open while they turned.

Background: one saturated color field in [ELECTRIC BLUE / BLOOD ORANGE], glowing brighter behind the head like a soft halo and fading to black at the edges. Dark oversized [COAT / HOODIE] melting into shadow.

Analog film look: heavy grain, faint horizontal scan lines, slight color bleed, deep crushed blacks. Moody editorial album-cover mood. No text, no logos.

How to review: Do the faces look pasted on? Ask for fewer echoes, lower opacity and alignment with the arc of the same rotation.

Customize the 9:16 template

In the fourth prompt, replace [ELECTRIC BLUE / BLOOD ORANGE] with ELECTRIC BLUE for electric blue or BLOOD ORANGE for blood orange. Replace [COAT / HOODIE] with COAT for a coat or HOODIE for a hooded sweatshirt. Remove the brackets and the option you did not choose.

In the tool, select vertical 9:16 format when available. In the first three prompts, add Vertical portrait, 9:16 composition. if you want to keep the same format across the series. 9:16 is an aspect ratio, not a required resolution.

Understand the intent behind camera terms

“Rim lighting” highlights the outline. “Scanlines” are horizontal scanning lines. “Pixel drag” means stretched pixels. “50mm f/1.8” and “85mm” are aesthetic cues in the prompt; they do not prove that a real lens, aperture or photographic capture was used.

Resolve conflicts with your reference

The scarlet prompt asks for messy hair; if that changes the person’s hairstyle, replace that sentence with Preserve the exact hairstyle from the reference photo. Red and amber form a warm color family here: avoid adding blue, green and violet in the same generation. The template asks for heavy grain; reduce it if it obscures the skin.

Guardrails to add

Keep the eyes, nose and lips of the main face sharp. Preserve the person's facial features, age and hairstyle. Use one dominant color family plus black. No busy background, props, bright accessories, text, logos or watermarks. Avoid flat, evenly lit beauty lighting; keep deep shadows.

Practice: build a series of four

Use the same reference for all four recipes. Keep the crop and compare results side by side. A series can vary the trail style while keeping the person and editorial intent recognizable.

  1. Start with cyan and approve the face.
  2. Return to the original reference to test violet, scarlet and blue.
  3. Choose the best result from each direction.
  4. Reject any image with altered facial features, even if the effect looks beautiful.
  5. Choose a use: an artist portrait, personal identity, poster or editorial study. Add text later in a layout editor.

Completion criteria

You are finished when every version keeps the face recognizable, uses one dominant color, has a directional trail and leaves the eyes, nose and mouth readable. If one version fails, correct that version; there is no need to remake the whole series.
